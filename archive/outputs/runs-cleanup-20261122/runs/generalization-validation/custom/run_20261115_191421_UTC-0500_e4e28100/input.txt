A typical neuron has a cell body, dendrites, and an axon. The cell body contains the nucleus, while dendrites receive many of the signals arriving from other neurons. The axon conducts action potentials toward its terminals, allowing the neuron to communicate with other cells.

An action potential involves a rapid change in the voltage across the neuron's membrane. Opening voltage-gated sodium channels allows sodium ions to enter the neuron during depolarization. The subsequent opening of voltage-gated potassium channels allows potassium ions to leave, contributing to repolarization. Myelin can increase the speed of action-potential conduction along an axon.

At a chemical synapse, an arriving action potential opens calcium channels in the presynaptic terminal. Calcium entry triggers the release of neurotransmitter from synaptic vesicles into the synaptic cleft. The neurotransmitter binds to receptors on the postsynaptic cell. Depending on the receptor, this binding can increase or decrease the likelihood of a postsynaptic action potential.
