Neurons are signaling cells that form neural circuits and neural networks. Each neuron has a soma, dendrites, and an axon. Electrical messages reach neurons in muscles and glands.

The soma contains the nucleus and most cytoplasm machinery producing and transporting essential proteins. Branched dendrites extend from the soma to receive other neurons' signals at synapses. An axon extends from the soma, carries an electrical signal, may branch before reaching terminals, and has lengths ranging from under a centimeter to over a meter. Axon terminals release chemical messengers (neurotransmitters) conveying signals across synapses.

Glial cells support neurons. Earlier estimates placed glia to neuron ratios at 10:1 or higher. Newer investigations suggest glia to neuron ratios are roughly 1:1 in some human and other primate brain regions, where these ratios vary considerably across regions.

The four major glial types occur in the central nervous system. Within this system, brain astrocyte networks regulate ion concentrations around neurons and supply nutrients. These networks also help control new neuronal connections. Additionally, microglia act as phagocytes mainly against infection and cellular damage, and they also regulate new connections.
