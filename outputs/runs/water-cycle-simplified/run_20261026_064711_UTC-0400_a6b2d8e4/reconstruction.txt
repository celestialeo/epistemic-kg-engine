The water cycle describes how water moves between the atmosphere, the land, and bodies of water. Solar energy supplies energy for evaporation from oceans, lakes, and moist ground, causing liquid water to change into water vapor. Condensation causes vapor to change into liquid. Oceans, lakes, rivers, soil, groundwater, snow, and ice all store water.

A plant emits vapor through leaf openings called stomata. Air cooling sufficiently enables air saturation, which can enable vapor condensation. A cloud contains tiny liquid droplets.

Some falling water evaporates before reaching the ground, while rain returns atmospheric water to the surface.

Precipitation reaches land. Water remains on a leaf within the leaf structure. Water flows over a surface within the surface area. Water enters the ground within the ground layer. Infiltration is defined as the entry of water through the ground surface into soil or other porous material. The rate of infiltration is influenced by soil structure, existing moisture, vegetation, and rainfall intensity. Deeper movement describes percolation.

Infiltrating water reaches the saturated zone, though not all of it does so. Water reaching the saturated zone contributes to groundwater recharge. An aquifer stores usable quantities of groundwater. Underground storage links to visible surface water via connections that allow groundwater to maintain flow in some streams during dry periods. A spring occurs where groundwater emerges at the surface.

Surface runoff develops when water flow over the land occurs, specifically including conditions where rainfall exceeds the ground's ability to absorb it or when the ground is already saturated. Rainfall exceeds the absorption capacity of the ground, leading to surface runoff. A river transports water downstream. A lake can temporarily store water and can influence the rate of timing of movement. A wetland can temporarily store water and can influence the rate of timing of movement. Snow and ice stores water. Melting releases liquid water into the environment. Liquid water can enter a stream or can enter soil.

A water budget describes input, output, and change in storage within a defined area and time interval. Water does not follow one compulsory loop. Human activity can act on balance. Paved surface often inhibits infiltration.
